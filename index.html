Monocular depth estimation

Judging distance from a single photograph.

A single photograph carries no direct measurement of distance. The model infers it from the cues a person uses without thinking: things that overlap sit in front, textures compress with range, parallel lines converge. Give it a picture and it returns a prediction for every pixel.

Reading the output

It is relative, not metric

The model predicts inverse relative depth. Bright means nearer and dark means further, but the scale is normalised per image. Nothing here tells you that a chair is 2.3 metres away, and two different photos are not comparable to each other. Absolute distance in metres requires a metric-trained variant.

Where it tends to fail

Mirrors and glass, because the cues describe the reflected scene rather than the surface. Large flat walls with no texture. Heavy motion blur. Aerial or macro framing far from the training distribution. Fine structures such as hair and wire fencing usually smear.

What happens to your photo

It is sent to a GPU container, held in memory for the duration of inference and discarded. Nothing is written to disk and nothing is stored. The container sleeps when idle, which is why the first request after a quiet period takes longer.
